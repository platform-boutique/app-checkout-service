# Known limitations

- **Compensation is best effort:** if shipping fails after the charge, one `Refund` is attempted (no retry). If it fails, the customer stays charged until someone refunds the transaction id found in the error log (the message says the refund is pending). Without an `idempotency_key` (and order storage) `PlaceOrder` is not idempotent: a client retry charges again.
- **A `Charge` that times out (10 s) is an ambiguous result:** checkout cannot know whether payment-service took the money. The call has no transaction id, so no `Refund` is possible, and payment-service has no idempotency key to ask again with. What checkout does is the same as for a decline: the stock is released, the order is marked `FAILED`, and the customer gets `UNAVAILABLE` ("payment service is temporarily unavailable, please retry"); the same `idempotency_key` then answers `FAILED_PRECONDITION`, a retry needs a new key. If payment-service did finish the charge after the deadline, the customer is charged for an order that is `FAILED`, and nothing finds that later (see No reconciliation). It shows up as `checkout_payments_total{outcome="unavailable"}` (a deadline counts as `unavailable`; a caller that gave up first counts as `canceled`), the `[PlaceOrder] charge failed` warning and an error span `saga charge`; payment-service's own log is the only place that tells whether the charge went through. Not run: a real slow payment-service (the deadline path is covered by a unit test with a fake that never answers).
- `Refund` needs a payment-service that implements it (payment-service main does: its proto has `Refund`, checked 2026-11-22); an older payment would answer `UNIMPLEMENTED` and the order would end with "refund is pending".
- The order-service integration was tested only against an in-process fake order-service (it re-implements the total rule with `big.Int`); it was **not run** against the real order-service or in the compose stack.
- **STOCK NOT COMMITTED:** if `Commit` fails 3 times (3 s per attempt, pauses of 1 s and 3 s) after charge and shipment, the order still succeeds and `STOCK NOT COMMITTED order=<id>` is logged at error level. The reservation then expires by the TTL of inventory-service (default 15 m) and that stock can be sold a second time. Nothing re-tries the commit later.
- **No reconciliation:** nothing compares orders, payments and reservations afterwards. Orders stuck `PENDING`, charges whose refund is pending, reservations that were never released or committed (`STOCK NOT RELEASED`) need manual follow-up from the logs. A reservation can also expire (TTL) while the card is being charged; `Commit` then fails and the stock may be oversold.
- **Idempotency across replicas needs order-service with `created`:** the in-memory guard against a concurrent double submit is per process. Across checkout replicas the race (both look the order up, both miss, both create) is closed by `Order.created` (`optional bool`, field 20) in the `CreateOrder` answer: only the call that inserted the row gets `true`, and checkout stops on `false` (PENDING -> `ABORTED`, PLACED or later -> stored result, FAILED -> `FAILED_PRECONDITION`) without reserving, charging or shipping. This holds only against an order-service that sends the field (order-service main does: `Order.created` is in its proto, checked 2026-11-22). Against an older order-service the field is unset, checkout treats that as unknown and falls back to the GetOrder lookup alone, so two replicas receiving the same key within milliseconds could both charge. Still open with the new order-service: an order stuck `PENDING` (crash, failing `ConfirmOrder` or `FailOrder`) answers `ABORTED` for its key forever (no reconciliation), and the guarantee was tested only with fakes (two `Service` instances sharing one fake order-service, and the real order-service's `created` against Postgres separately), never with two real checkout replicas. A sequential retry is always safe (it goes through the stored order). Without `ORDER_SERVICE_ADDR` there is no idempotency.
- The inventory integration was tested only against in-process fake order-, payment-, shipping- and inventory-services; it was **not run** against the real inventory-service or in the compose stack.
- **ORDER NOT CONFIRMED:** `ConfirmOrder` is tried 3 times (3 s per attempt, pauses of 0.5 s and 2 s, detached context). If all fail, `ORDER NOT CONFIRMED order=<id>` is logged at error level, the customer still gets the success (charged and shipped) and the order stays `PENDING` in order-service: it is not in the customer's order history, its key answers `ABORTED`, and nothing re-tries or reconciles it later; it needs manual follow-up from the log.
- **EVENT NOT PUBLISHED (no outbox):** with `KAFKA_BROKERS` set the `orders.placed` event is published inside the request, best effort, with a 5 s bound. If the broker is down or slow the order still succeeds, `EVENT NOT PUBLISHED order=<id>` is logged at error level and nothing publishes the event later. Consequences: order-service's consumer cannot reconcile that order, and since checkout does not call email-service when Kafka is on, the customer gets **no confirmation email**. A crash between the commit of the stock and the publish loses the event the same way. A timed-out publish may still reach the broker later (at-least-once; consumers are idempotent by `event_id`).
- The Kafka producer was tested with a fake publisher and against a single throwaway `apache/kafka:4.3.2` node (publish and read back, same key on one partition, broker unreachable); it was **not run** in the compose stack, against a broker that goes away mid-run, with TLS/SASL (the compose broker is plaintext without auth) or with several checkout replicas.
- Health is static; readiness does not reflect downstream availability.
- The image is `linux/amd64` only (owner decision 2026-11-14) and runs as the numeric user `10001:10001`.
- Retries exist only for `Commit`, `Release` and `ConfirmOrder` (3 attempts each; the Kafka publish is one attempt chain bounded by 5 s); every other call is a single attempt with its own deadline (3 s for cart, catalog, currency and the shipping quote, 10 s for `Charge`, 5 s for `ShipOrder` and the e-mail call; a shorter deadline of the caller wins, see Deadlines).
